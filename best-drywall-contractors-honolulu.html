Detailed breakdown of each option

1. Hire a licensed drywall specialist

Best for: quality-critical work Type: direct hire

What sets it apart: A drywall specialist holds a C-33 specialty license in Hawaii, meaning drywall is their primary trade rather than a side service. They own the tools, know the material suppliers on-island, and understand how Oahu's humidity affects joint compound drying times and tape adhesion.

Example: H Drywall, LLC is a Honolulu-based C-33 licensed contractor with 30+ years of experience, a BBB A+ rating, and top reviews on Angi, Yelp, and HomeAdvisor. They specialize in residential live-in renovations, texture matching, and water damage repair across Oahu.

Strengths:

  • Highest finish quality, especially Level 4 and Level 5 finishes
  • Deep knowledge of Hawaii-specific materials and conditions
  • Direct communication with no middleman or platform fees
  • Can advise on whether permits are needed for your specific project

Limitations:

  • Fewer drywall specialists exist on Oahu compared to the mainland, so scheduling can take longer
  • Cannot handle plumbing, electrical, or painting (you will need separate trades for a full remodel)
  • You need to verify licensing yourself through the Hawaii DCCA website

Choose if: You want the best possible finish, your project is primarily drywall work, and you are willing to coordinate other trades separately.

2. Use Angi (formerly Angie's List)

Best for: verified reviews Type: online platform

What sets it apart: Angi verifies contractor credentials and collects structured reviews with ratings for quality, responsiveness, punctuality, and value. The platform serves the Honolulu market well and includes pricing estimates for common drywall projects.

Strengths:

  • Verified reviews are harder to fake than on open platforms
  • Shows specific rating categories so you can compare on what matters to you
  • Cost estimates give you a baseline before requesting quotes

Limitations:

  • National cost data does not reflect Hawaii's higher material and labor rates
  • Some contractors pay for premium placement, which can influence visibility
  • Smaller Oahu-only contractors may have fewer reviews than mainland companies

Choose if: You want to compare contractors by verified ratings and do not mind contacting them through the platform.

3. Search Google Reviews and Google Maps

Best for: quick local comparison Type: search engine

What sets it apart: Searching "drywall contractor near me" on Google Maps shows contractors with their ratings, review counts, phone numbers, and locations on a map. It is the fastest way to see who is operating in your specific Oahu neighborhood.

Strengths:

  • Free and instant, no account or sign-up required
  • Shows location proximity so you can find contractors close to your project
  • Review volume and recency are easy to check at a glance

Limitations:

  • Google does not verify contractor licenses or insurance
  • Review quality varies widely and fake reviews exist
  • Some good contractors have minimal Google presence, especially older established companies

Choose if: You want a quick overview of who is active in your area and plan to verify credentials on your own.

4. Check HomeAdvisor / Angi Leads

Best for: getting multiple quotes Type: lead service

What sets it apart: HomeAdvisor (now part of Angi) works as a lead-generation service. You describe your project, and the platform matches you with prescreened contractors who then reach out with quotes. This saves time if you want to compare prices without calling multiple companies.

Strengths:

  • Contractors are background-checked and prescreened
  • You get multiple bids without searching individually
  • Good for understanding the price range for your specific project

Limitations:

  • Contractors pay per lead, which can be reflected in their pricing
  • You may get calls from contractors you are not interested in
  • Fewer drywall-specific contractors available on Oahu compared to mainland markets

Choose if: You want to compare 2-3 quotes quickly and prefer contractors to come to you rather than the other way around.

5. Hire a general contractor

Best for: multi-trade remodels Type: direct hire

What sets it apart: A general contractor (B license in Hawaii) manages your entire renovation and subcontracts drywall to a specialist. This makes sense when drywall is just one part of a bigger project involving plumbing, electrical, framing, or painting.

Strengths:

  • Single point of contact for multi-trade projects
  • GC handles scheduling, coordination, and permitting
  • Useful when you do not want to manage multiple contractors yourself

Limitations:

  • GC markup adds 15-25% on top of the drywall subcontractor's price
  • You have less control over which drywall sub does the actual work
  • For drywall-only projects, a GC adds cost without adding value

Choose if: Your project involves multiple trades and you want one contractor managing everything. Avoid for drywall-only jobs where you would pay a markup for coordination you do not need.

6. Ask for word-of-mouth referrals

Best for: trust and accountability Type: personal referral

What sets it apart: Hawaii's communities are tightly connected. Asking neighbors, coworkers, your building's property manager, or local Facebook groups (like "Oahu Homeowners" or neighborhood-specific groups) often surfaces contractors who do excellent work but do not invest in online marketing.

Strengths:

  • Referrals come with built-in accountability because reputation matters in a small community
  • You can see the actual work in your friend's or neighbor's home
  • Often surfaces contractors who are fully booked through referrals alone, which is itself a quality signal

Limitations:

  • Limited to your personal network's experience
  • No structured way to compare options or verify credentials
  • Your friend's small patch job does not tell you much about a contractor's ability to do a full room

Choose if: You trust your network and want a contractor who has already proven themselves to someone you know.

7. Search Yelp for drywall contractors

Best for: photo-heavy research Type: online platform

What sets it apart: Yelp listings in Honolulu tend to include detailed written reviews and before/after photos uploaded by both contractors and customers. This makes it useful when you want to visually assess the quality of a contractor's work before reaching out.

Strengths:

  • Photo galleries let you see actual completed work
  • Long-form reviews often describe the full customer experience, not just a star rating
  • Active Honolulu market with a reasonable number of drywall-related listings

Limitations:

  • Yelp's review filter sometimes hides legitimate reviews, frustrating both contractors and customers
  • Contractors who do not pay for Yelp ads may appear lower in results
  • No license or insurance verification built into the platform

Choose if: You want to see photos of finished work and read detailed reviews before making contact.

Decision framework

Use these guidelines to match your situation to the right approach.

Specialist vs. general contractor

  • Choose a drywall specialist if your project is primarily drywall work (repairs, retexturing, new installation in one area) and you want the highest finish quality. Specialists cost less than going through a GC for drywall-only jobs.
  • Choose a general contractor if your project involves multiple trades (demo, framing, electrical, plumbing, drywall, paint) and you want one person managing the whole job. The GC markup is worth it when coordination complexity is high.

Online platforms vs. direct contact

  • Use Angi or HomeAdvisor if you are new to Oahu, do not have a local network, and want the platform to do initial screening for you.
  • Use Google or Yelp if you prefer to do your own research, read reviews, and reach out directly.
  • Ask for referrals if you have lived on Oahu long enough to have a trusted network and want the strongest accountability signal.

DIY vs. hiring out

  • DIY makes sense if you are patching a single small hole (under 6 inches), the wall is not in a high-visibility area, and you have basic tools. A patch kit from Home Depot or City Mill costs $15-$30.
  • Hire a pro if the damage is larger than a dinner plate, involves water damage, affects multiple areas, requires texture matching to existing walls, or is in a visible living space. In Hawaii, botched DIY drywall work often costs more to fix than hiring a professional in the first place because the humid climate makes mistakes harder to hide.
  • Always hire a pro if the project involves removing existing drywall that could contain asbestos (common in Hawaii homes built before 1980), requires a permit, or involves mold remediation behind the drywall.

Hawaii-specific considerations

  • Budget 20-40% more than mainland estimates. Every sheet of drywall, bucket of mud, and roll of tape ships to Hawaii by container. Labor rates reflect the higher cost of living on Oahu.
  • Ask about humidity planning. Joint compound takes longer to dry in Hawaii, especially November through March. A contractor who schedules around weather patterns will deliver a better result than one who rushes dry times.
  • Verify licensing at the Hawaii DCCA website. Search the contractor's name or license number at cca.hawaii.gov/pvl. Look for an active C-33 (drywall/plastering) or B (general) license. An unlicensed contractor doing work over $1,500 in Hawaii is breaking the law.
  • Ask about material sourcing. Some Oahu contractors keep common drywall sizes in their warehouse, avoiding project delays from supply chain issues. Others order per job, which can add days or weeks to your timeline.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about drywall costs, permits, and timelines in Hawaii.

How much does drywall cost in Hawaii?

Drywall work in Hawaii typically costs 20-40% more than on the mainland. A standard 4x8 sheet of drywall runs $18-$28 in Honolulu compared to $10-$15 on the mainland, due to shipping costs. Labor rates for drywall installation range from $2.50-$4.50 per square foot installed, and finishing adds $1.50-$3.00 per square foot depending on the level of finish. A typical bedroom (roughly 500 sq ft of wall and ceiling area) costs $2,500-$5,000 for full installation and finishing in Honolulu.

Do I need a permit for drywall work in Honolulu?

Cosmetic drywall repairs like patching holes, fixing cracks, or retexturing generally do not require a permit in Honolulu. However, permits are required when drywall work is part of a larger renovation that involves structural changes, electrical or plumbing modifications, or converting a room's use. The City and County of Honolulu Department of Planning and Permitting (DPP) handles building permits. Your contractor should be able to tell you whether your specific project requires one.

How long does drywall installation take in Honolulu?

A single room typically takes 3-5 days from hanging to final finish, including drying time between coats. Larger projects like a full home renovation can take 2-4 weeks for the drywall phase. Hawaii's humidity can extend drying times for joint compound, especially during wet months (November through March). Professional contractors in Honolulu account for this by scheduling around weather patterns and using appropriate ventilation.

What is a C-33 license in Hawaii and why does it matter?

The C-33 is Hawaii's specialty contractor license for drywall and plastering work. Contractors who hold this license have passed exams specific to drywall installation, finishing, and repair. While a general contractor (B license) can also do drywall work, a C-33 holder specializes in it. Hiring a C-33 licensed contractor means you are working with someone whose primary trade is drywall, which typically results in better finish quality, especially for Level 4 and Level 5 finishes.
